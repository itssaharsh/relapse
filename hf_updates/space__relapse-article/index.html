Relapse: an outdated tokenizer setting overstated unlearning at the default layers, not at layers 3 to 5, in post-trained models

Small models like Cohere Labs' Tiny Aya run offline, on phones and laptops, where no server can filter what they say. Any safety has to live in the model's weights, and it has to survive the way the model is deployed: the compressed file people download, and the tokenizer setting their copy loads. We used four Lexsi Labs tools to make Tiny Aya forget hazardous biology and checked both. The paper is An Outdated Tokenizer Setting Overstated Unlearning Applied at Layers 5–7 in 16 of 16 Runs, but Not at Layers 3–5 in Post-Trained Models.

In brief

An outdated tokenizer setting made the unlearning look deeper than it was. Runs unlearned and audited through it at SafeTune's default layers gave back about half of the audited forgetting (47%) once loaded through the official tokenizer, in 16 of 16 runs that count, and every one of them had also lost more than 3 points of general knowledge where it was audited. At layers 3 to 5 the same change did nothing on the four post-trained variants (0 of 16). The layers are not shown to be the cause: each window had its own learning rates.

Correction (November 21, 2026)

Our Stage 1 results contained a serious error. We first withdrew two of the claims in our submission, and have since withdrawn every Stage 1 score.

What happened. Stage 1 ran on Kaggle's copy of Tiny Aya. That copy predates a fix Cohere made on Hugging Face on September 30, 2026: under transformers v5, its tokenizer setting splits text differently from the way the model was trained. All 1,273 WMDP-bio prompts and all 1,000 MMLU prompts were affected, in both training and scoring. The model weights themselves are fine.

How we confirmed it. We scored the same weights, in the same job, with both tokenizer settings:

Model WMDP-bio, outdated setting WMDP-bio, official tokenizer
Original 63.3% 63.3%
RMU at the default layers (5 to 7) 38.7% 47.5%
RMU at the CircuitKIT layers (7 to 9) 36.8% 35.7%
RMU at layers 10 to 12 41.6% 52.2%

What we withdraw:

  • The relapse. We reported that an edit at layers 10 to 12 looked unlearned at full precision and regained 7.4 points in a 4-bit file. With the official tokenizer that model barely forgets (52.2%) and its 4-bit files do not rise (+0.3 points in Q4_0, p = 0.74; +0.7 in Q4_K_M, p = 0.47).
  • The repair. Training on the 4-bit file had no real relapse to repair, and that model scores 58.1% with the official tokenizer.
  • Everything built on those two: that relapse is format-specific, and that it comes from rounding the layers in front of the edit.
  • Every other Stage 1 number (the sweeps, extra seeds, languages, prompt and fine-tuning attacks, Tiny Aya Fire and Earth) was measured with the outdated setting. We do not rely on any of it until it is re-run.

The original write-up is kept, marked as superseded, in docs/archive/, and its raw runs in results/raw/.

Results (Stage 2: pre-registered, official tokenizer)

Every experiment below was registered in this repository before it ran (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6), and every hypothesis that failed is listed. Every Stage 2 job downloaded the main branch of CohereLabs/tiny-aya-<variant> when it started; by the job start times and the repositories' history that was the revision carrying Cohere's tokenizer fix (Global af89d219b5, Fire 73500c80fc, Earth 0624fafbe2, Water 41a99216d1, Base d62ac17e24, all committed September 30, 2026), as the paper's appendix sets out.

1. Forgetting usually survives the 4-bit file. In a map of 150 runs across the five Tiny Aya models, 107 of the 109 runs that forgot enough to count kept their forgetting in both Q4_0 and NF4.

2. But one file can relapse while its siblings hold. On three models, one 4-bit format brought back at least 5 points of hazardous knowledge while the other formats of the same weights did not:

Tiny Aya Base, layers 10 to 12, seed 43 41.6% 46.8% (+5.3) relapse 37.9% (-3.6) 46.2% (+4.5) 39.6% (-2.1)
Tiny Aya Fire, layers 13 to 15, seed 44 34.8% 33.2% (-1.6) 40.1% (+5.3) relapse 33.1% (-1.8) 36.6% (+1.7)
Tiny Aya Water, layers 10 to 12, seed 42 38.6% 42.3% (+3.7) 46.3% (+7.7) relapse 41.2% (+2.5) 37.2% (-1.4)
  • The Base relapse reproduced when the seed-12 runs were retrained, and happened again on 1 of 8 fresh seeds. The Fire and Water cases are one run each, never retrained, and we treat them as exploratory; by an exact McNemar test with a Holm correction over the cells screened with them they are not noise (scripts/mcnemar_holm.py).
  • Loaded in 4-bit through bitsandbytes, the way NF4 is really used, Fire and Water gave the same rises as above.
  • Scored inside llama.cpp's own runtime on 600 questions (an exploratory check on the first Base case), the answers matched our scoring on at least 99.8% of questions at full precision. On those questions Q4_0 raised the original model by +3.5 points and the unlearned one by +3.2.
  • No real Q4_K_M file relapsed. In every case at least one 4-bit format held, so scoring each file before release catches it.
  • It is rare and modest, and on Base part of it is the format, not the unlearning: the original Tiny Aya Base also gains about 1.8 points in Q4_0. Net of that, no Base run reaches 5 points, and the typical Base run moves no more than the original does. Fire and Water are the cases that survive this check (their originals fall slightly in NF4).
  • The 5-point line is a choice. Two of the three real Base files rose 4.5 and 4.6 points, just under it.

3. The rise comes from weights the edit never touched. Rounding only the three edited matrices adds at most 1.7 points in any relapsing run. On Water and Fire the rise comes from the layers in front of the edit (+7.6 of +7.7 points on Water). On Base seed 12 it comes from the layers behind it.

4. Training on the file closes the gap, at a cost. In the 5 relapsing runs, the retrained model no longer relapsed in 5, was significantly safer in the file in 4, and stayed within 3 MMLU points in 3. But plain RMU trained for the same extra time did almost as well (4, 4 and 3 of 5), so we cannot separate the repair from longer training, and too much of either forgets far more than intended.

5. A different tokenizer setting is the larger risk. Of 45 effective runs unlearned through the outdated tokenizer setting, 33 scored higher through the official one and 12 lower; 29 rose by at least 5 points. The average rise was +13.3 points at SafeTune's default layers 5 to 7 and +14.1 at layers 10 to 12, but -0.6 at layers 7 to 9. Every Tiny Aya Base run trained this way scored 24.9% (chance) in its own pipeline and 31.2% to 60.9% through the official tokenizer. The direction matters: runs unlearned through the official tokenizer and scored through the outdated one rose by only +1.4 points on average (6 of 47 relapsed).

Default layers against layers 3 to 5, at the learning rates tested. In a further registered test on new seeds, runs unlearned through the outdated tokenizer at SafeTune's default layers relapsed in 16 of 16 cases (+11.4 points on average). At layers 3 to 5, the window we release, 0 of 16 relapsed (+1.2 on average), and at layers 7 to 9, 0 of 16. Scored through the official tokenizer, the layers 3 to 5 runs sit at 36.8% and the default-layer runs at 50.8%. That is on the four post-trained variants. Tiny Aya Base is the exception, and our rule for which runs count removes it: at every window it drops to guessing level in the outdated pipeline and comes back up through the official one, at layers 3 to 5 by 8.5 to 11.6 points. Counting every run, 20 of 20 rose by 5 points or more at the default layers (mean rise +12.1), 4 of 20 at layers 3 to 5 (+3.0) and 4 of 20 at layers 7 to 9 (+1.9); outside the default layers all of them are Base. The effect was measured on multiple-choice prompts, whose line breaks and digit groups are where the two settings split text differently.

What came back was not only the hazard (an exploratory reading of the same runs). Where they were audited, through the outdated tokenizer, the default-layer runs had also lost 5.8 MMLU points, none of them within our 3-point line; through the official tokenizer they lost 1.7. At layers 3 to 5 the loss was 0.6 and 1.1. Tiny Aya Base scored 23.1% to 24.0% on MMLU as audited: the edit had wrecked it there, not unlearned it. Over both tokenizer experiments, runs trained through the outdated setting that had lost 3 or more MMLU points as audited rose by 5 points or more in 72 of 72 cases, and the others in 0 of 48. About half of the audited forgetting came back (47%; 35% to 57% per run), and the deployed runs land where runs unlearned through the official tokenizer at the same layers, seeds and learning rates land (50.9% against 52.1%): the outdated audit overstated the forgetting, it did not lose real forgetting at load. By model and seed the count is 8 of 8 at the default layers against 0 of 8 at layers 3 to 5. So the layers are confounded with how much the edit damaged the model in the pipeline it was audited in, and the pattern is not ordered by depth: layers 10 to 12 relapsed like the default layers, layers 7 to 9 held. General knowledge lost in the audit is a warning sign, not a test: the rule is fitted on these runs, and our Stage 1 model at layers 10 to 12 had lost only 1.5 MMLU points where it was audited and still rose 10.6.

What the two settings do to the text (counted without any model, scripts/token_differences.py). They give different tokens for all 1,273 WMDP-bio prompts and all 1,000 MMLU prompts, and for 496 of the WMDP-bio questions without the multiple-choice layout. The outdated setting splits every digit apart, separates punctuation from the word it joins, and loses the pieces that join punctuation to a line break.

It was absent on single-line prompts. The effect above was measured on prompts with the question and each option on its own line. In a sixth registered test we scored the same 60 saved runs again with every line break removed, through both settings (nothing was trained). The default-layer runs then score 46.6% through the outdated setting and 47.4% through the official one: a rise of +0.8 points against +11.4 in the multiple-choice layout, and 0 of 16 relapse (we had expected at least half to; that hypothesis failed). No run had lost 3 MMLU points through the outdated setting, and Tiny Aya Base is not at guessing level there. The agreement is not by construction: the two settings still split 815 of the 1,273 single-line WMDP-bio prompts differently, and on those alone the default-layer runs rise by +1.2 points, against +11.7 on the same questions in the multiple-choice layout (scripts/single_line_tokens.py, exploratory). So the overstated audit was absent in the one layout without line breaks that we tested, which also joins the options with |; other layouts are untested and why is not explained. (In results/single_line/summary.json the field check_registered_layout_max_difference, 2.6 points, also includes the original Base model's own shift between the two settings; over the rescored runs the registered scores are reproduced exactly.)

The outdated setting is still widespread. The two settings are two public copies of the same model: Cohere changed tokenizer_class on Hugging Face on September 30, 2026, and copies made before that keep the old value. On 2026-11-26 we read the tokenizer file, or the GGUF header, of every public copy we could find, without downloading weights (scripts/check_tokenizer.py, results/survey/): 1,275 Hugging Face repositories tied to a Tiny Aya model, and Kaggle Models. Cohere's five repositories carry the official setting. All 5 Kaggle variations carry the outdated one. Of 915 derived Hugging Face copies whose setting could be read, 423 carry it: 5 were our own Stage 1 models, which we corrected on November 26, 2026 (UTC), and the other 418, mostly fine-tunes and adapters, have 64,619 downloads (7,133 in the last 30 days). Loaded with transformers 5.20.2, 12 of 12 sampled copies split a test prompt differently from the official tokenizer. GGUF files are nearly all safe (2 of 77 name another pre-tokenizer). This counts exposure, not harm: we saw the relapse only in our own runs, 390 of those copies were last modified before the fix and 28 after it, none is named as an unlearning model and 6 are named as safety-tuned, and which direction of mismatch a copy faces depends on the transformers version it was trained with, which its files do not record. Who is exposed is anyone who unlearns or audits on line-broken multiple-choice prompts through a copy with the outdated setting and ships a model that users load through the official one.

6. Where to edit. Two registered tests compared layers 3 to 5 with SafeTune's default layers; one passed and one narrowly missed. In the first, layers 3 to 5 forgot more within 3 MMLU points in 9 of 10 comparisons (p = 0.011). In the second, on fresh seeds, they forgot 5.6 to 13.3 points more on four of the five models (8 of 10 comparisons, p = 0.055, short of our own line). What placement buys is more forgetting per MMLU point (20.8 hazard points for 1.6 MMLU points, against 14.6 for 2.3). Layers 3 to 5 also did not relapse through the tokenizer where the default layers did, but the default-layer runs were the ones whose audit showed MMLU damage, so the layer is not shown to be the reason. Placement does not buy selectivity within biology: benign biology falls by 6.3 points against 4.5, the same 3.30 and 3.26 hazard points forgotten per point of benign biology lost. CircuitKIT ranks layers 3 to 5 first on three models and first when its attribution is pooled over the five; its pick for each model alone was unstable and that hypothesis failed (with 160 prompts instead of 64, an exploratory repeat, the per-model pick changed on three of five models while the pooled pick stayed layers 3 to 5).

Method

Unlearning. SafeTune's RMU edits the MLP output projection of three consecutive layers. On hazardous text it pushes activations toward a fixed random vector; on ordinary text (Wikitext) it holds them to a frozen copy of the model. CircuitKIT's EAP-IG attribution, pooled over the five models, ranks layers 3 to 5 first; SafeTune's default is 5 to 7.

Scoring. Each question is scored from the next-token probabilities of A to D, so no judge model is involved. Runs are compared on the same questions with a paired bootstrap; AuditKIT does this for every real file. Reworded and translated questions pass CuratorKIT's quality gates.

Formats. Real files are built with llama.cpp and read back for scoring. The Q4_0 and NF4 round trips are exact copies of what those formats store for the decoder's linear weights, with the embedding table left at fp16.

Limits

  • Knowledge is measured by multiple choice, not open generation. The tokenizer effect belongs to the multiple-choice layout: with single-line prompts it is absent, and we have no mechanism for it.
  • Layers 3 to 5 did not relapse through the tokenizer on the four post-trained variants only (Tiny Aya Base rose at every window), and the default-layer runs that did relapse were the ones whose audit showed MMLU damage, so the layer is not shown to be the cause.
  • Relapses in 4-bit files are rare, so each rests on one or two seeds per model; the Fire and Water cases are one run each.
  • Placement buys more forgetting per MMLU point, not per point of benign biology.
  • RMU hides knowledge more than it removes it: a short fine-tune on the topic brings much of it back.
  • The forgetting also costs benign biology (see the tables above).
  • One model family and one unlearning method.
